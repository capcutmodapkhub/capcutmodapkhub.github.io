CapCut for Instagram Reels

Reels reward polish: clean framing, readable captions and a hook that lands instantly. Here is how to edit Reels in CapCut that look like they came from a studio, not a phone.

Reels are not TikToks: know the difference

Reels and TikTok look similar, but the audiences behave differently. Reels viewers skew slightly older and expect a bit more polish; a video that works on TikTok will usually work on Reels, but the reverse is not always true. Instagram also crops your video in the feed preview — the grid shows only the middle square — so your subject and text must survive a square crop as well as the full vertical view. Plan your framing for both: keep the subject centred and the text in the middle band of the frame.

Safe zones: the parts Instagram covers

Instagram covers more of your video than TikTok does. The top shows your username and the follow button, the right edge holds the like, comment and share buttons, and the bottom holds the caption, the audio credit and the action bar. Keep all important text and faces inside the middle 70 percent of the frame. Before exporting, preview your video with these overlays in mind: if a caption sits under the like button, move it up. A simple test is to hold your thumb over the right edge and the bottom fifth of the screen — whatever is still readable is safe.

Pacing for Reels

Reels can be slightly slower than TikTok — viewers tolerate a two-second establishing shot where TikTok would punish it — but the first three seconds still decide everything. Open with the outcome or the question, then deliver the content in short, clear steps. Cut pauses and filler words, but do not cut so fast that the video feels anxious; Reels audiences respond well to a calm, confident pace with one visual change every 2–4 seconds. Use a zoom punch or a caption change, not a new transition, to mark each beat.

Captions and text style for Reels

Use auto captions in a clean, bold font — white text with a dark outline or a subtle background box reads best on Instagram's bright interface. Keep captions in the lower-middle of the frame, above the caption bar. For the hook text, use a larger size and a different colour so it reads as a title, not a subtitle. Avoid tiny text: Instagram compresses video more aggressively than TikTok, and small text turns to mush. If a caption is hard to read at arm's length on your phone, it is too small.

Music and trending audio on Reels

Instagram's music library is separate from TikTok's, and using a trending Instagram sound helps the algorithm place your Reel. In CapCut, edit with a placeholder track for timing, then add the real trending audio inside Instagram when you post — that attaches the sound correctly and counts toward the trend. If your video has a voiceover, keep the music bed low (around 10–15 percent volume) so the voice stays clear. And be careful with copyrighted music on business accounts: Instagram mutes or restricts some tracks for commercial profiles, so use the royalty-free library if you post for a brand.

Exporting for Reels

Export at 1080 × 1920, 30fps, high bitrate (12–20 Mbps), MP4. Instagram accepts up to 90 seconds for Reels, but 30–60 seconds performs best for most niches. After exporting, upload directly in the Instagram app rather than through a third-party scheduler when you can — native uploads sometimes get better reach and keep the audio attachment intact. Add your caption, hashtags and cover frame inside Instagram, not in CapCut, so they stay editable later.

Frequently asked questions

What size should Reels be?

1080 × 1920 vertical (9:16). Instagram also shows a square crop in the feed grid, so keep your subject centred.

Why do my Reels look blurry?

Instagram re-compresses uploads. Export at high bitrate from CapCut and upload on Wi-Fi, and check that "Upload at highest quality" is enabled in Instagram settings.

Can I post the same video to TikTok and Reels?

Yes, but remove the TikTok watermark first — Instagram deprioritises videos with a visible TikTok logo. Export a clean copy from CapCut for each platform.
