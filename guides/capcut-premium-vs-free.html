CapCut Premium vs Free: What's Actually Worth Paying For?

CapCut's free version is already a serious editor. Premium adds extra effects, templates and cloud space — but for most creators, the free version is enough. Here is the honest comparison.

What the free version includes

The free version of CapCut includes the full core editor: multi-track timeline, trimming and splitting, speed controls, transitions, filters, text and stickers, auto captions, keyframe animation, background removal, and export up to 4K on most devices. You also get a large library of free templates, effects and music. For TikTok, Reels and Shorts, this is genuinely enough to make professional-looking videos. Many creators with large audiences edit entirely on the free version and never pay anything.

What premium adds

Premium — sold as CapCut Pro — unlocks the items marked with a crown or "Pro" badge: extra filters and effects, premium templates, more fonts and stickers, additional cloud storage, and some AI tools such as advanced background removal and higher-quality auto captions in more languages. It also removes the small premium watermark that appears when you use certain pro assets. The exact list changes over time as new effects are added and old ones move between tiers, so check the in-app store before deciding.

Templates and effects: the real difference

The most visible difference is templates. Free templates are plentiful and cover the popular styles, but the newest trending templates are often premium first. The same applies to effects: the free library has hundreds of filters and effects, while premium adds the newest and most polished ones. If you edit daily and always want the latest trending look, premium saves time. If you edit a few times a week, the free library is more than enough — and you can recreate most premium looks with keyframes and manual adjustments.

Cloud storage and working across devices

Premium includes more cloud storage for your projects, which matters if you edit on both phone and desktop and want projects to sync. Free accounts get a smaller allowance, and large projects can fill it quickly. If you only edit on one phone, this does not matter at all — your projects live on your device. Creators who work in a team or switch devices often will feel this difference; solo phone editors will not.

Price and value

Premium is a monthly or yearly subscription, priced differently by country, and it is billed through the app store — not through any third-party site. A yearly plan usually costs much less per month than paying monthly. Before paying, try this test: edit for two weeks on the free version and note every time you hit a paywall. If it happens once or twice, skip premium. If it happens every session and it slows you down, the subscription will probably pay for itself in saved time.

Who should pay — and who should not

Pay if you post daily, run a client or business account, or use CapCut as your main work tool. Do not pay if you edit occasionally, are still learning, or mostly use the same few effects. Beginners should especially stay free for the first month: learning the editor matters far more than unlocking extra filters. And never pay through a random website promising "premium for free" — that is not a subscription, it is a modified app with real risks, as our safety guide explains.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use CapCut professionally without premium?

Yes. The free editor exports up to 4K and includes everything needed for client work: captions, keyframes, transitions and colour tools.

Does premium remove the CapCut watermark?

There is no watermark on normal exports. Premium only removes the watermark that appears when you use certain pro assets in a project.

Is there a free trial of premium?

CapCut sometimes offers a free trial in the app. Any trial comes from inside the official app — never from a third-party site.
