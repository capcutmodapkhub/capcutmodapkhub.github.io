Background Removal in CapCut

Removing a background used to need a green screen and a desktop editor. In CapCut it takes one tap — if you know which tool to use and how to clean up the edges.

Auto cutout: one-tap background removal

Select your video clip, tap "Cutout" in the bottom toolbar, then "Auto cutout". CapCut analyses the clip and removes the background, leaving just the person or subject. It works best when the subject is clearly separated from the background — a person standing against a plain wall, for example. Once cut out, you can place a new background behind the subject: another video, a photo, or a solid colour. This is how creators film in a messy room and appear to stand in front of a beach, a city skyline or a branded backdrop.

Custom cutout: when auto cutout misses

Auto cutout sometimes keeps the wrong thing — a chair, a plant, part of the background that looks like a person. When that happens, use "Custom cutout" instead: you draw around the subject with your finger, and CapCut keeps only what you outlined. It is slower, but it works on objects and animals that auto cutout does not recognise, and on shots where two people overlap. Trace slowly and slightly inside the edge of the subject; a small margin is better than including background, because you can soften the edge afterwards.

Chroma key: the green screen method

If you film in front of a green (or blue) screen, chroma key gives the cleanest result of all. Select the clip, tap "Cutout", then "Chroma key", and tap the green colour in the preview. Adjust the intensity slider until the green disappears without eating into the subject, and the shadow slider to remove leftover green spill around hair and edges. Chroma key beats auto cutout for hair, transparent objects and fast movement — but it needs even lighting on the screen, with no shadows or wrinkles, or the key will leave green patches.

Cleaning up edges

The difference between an amateur cutout and a professional one is the edges. After removing the background, zoom into the preview and look at the hair and the outline of the subject. If the edge looks hard and blocky, increase the feather or edge softness slightly. If there is a coloured fringe — usually green or the colour of the old background — use the "remove spill" or edge adjustment control if available. A tiny bit of the new background showing through hair is normal; a thick halo is not. When in doubt, choose a background that is similar in brightness to the original, because a bright subject on a dark background shows every edge flaw.

Background blur and replacement ideas

You do not always need to replace the background completely. The "background blur" effect keeps your real background but blurs it, which looks professional and hides a messy room while keeping the shot natural. For replacement, match the light: if your subject was filmed in warm indoor light, a cold blue beach background will look fake. Add a slight shadow under the subject's feet and match the colour grade of both layers with the same filter, and the composite becomes convincing. Product videos, tutorials and reaction videos all use this technique daily.

Common problems and fixes

If auto cutout flickers — the background appears and disappears between frames — the subject is moving too fast or the lighting changes during the clip. Try chroma key instead, or split the clip and cut out the stable sections separately. If the cutout removes part of the subject, such as an arm against a similar-coloured wall, use custom cutout for that section. And if the export looks worse than the preview, raise the export bitrate: cutout edges are the first thing compression destroys, so a high-bitrate export matters more here than anywhere else.

Frequently asked questions

Is background removal free in CapCut?

Auto cutout and chroma key are included in the free version. Some advanced cutout options are premium.

Can I remove the background from a photo in CapCut?

Yes — the same cutout tools work on photos. Import the photo, tap Cutout, and choose auto or custom.

Why does my cutout look bad around hair?

Hair is the hardest part. Use chroma key with a green screen if you can, or soften the edge slightly and choose a background close in colour to the hair.
